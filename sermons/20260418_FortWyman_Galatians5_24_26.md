## Crucify the Flesh

Who here wants to be an executioner? Like, the medieval, black hood, large axe… Nobody? Neither do I. Yet, that’s what we are called to do to our flesh.

We who have surrendered our lives to the authority of King Jesus, have taken on the role of executioner and have crucified our flesh. Our new lives in Christ demands us to live differently than those who live outside of Christ. This passage wrestles with the war between our fleshly desires and our spiritual desires. Let’s back up to verse 16 for context.

**Read 5:16-21**  
I don’t know about you but that sounds so awful. However, this is our natural state if we follow our fleshly desires. So why are we surprised when non-Christians act like this?

**Read 5:22-23**  
This sounds amazing\! This is the place I want to be and be around. However, this is not our natural state. There is a song I like called Angelz by Shai Linne that has a line 

"*I've got too much of the world in me to enjoy the Lord*  
*And too much of the Lord in me to enjoy the world*”.

This war can only be won by crucifying our flesh. What does it mean to crucify the flesh? Crucifixion was a capital punishment designed to be as socially shameful and painful as possible for as long as possible. Let us dig into our job as an executioner. 

First, we must strip the victim naked so they are completely exposed. We must take a hard, honest look at our flesh and expose everything. Don’t leave anything covered up. Sin is ugly, messy, and shameful and must be exposed. If you need help, read this (*Hold Bible)*. God’s Word is the light that exposes our darkness.

Second, Crucifixions must be public. We can’t do any of this alone. Find a group of Godly men and women who you can confess your sins to and share one another’s burdens. Our God is relational and so are we. I’m not saying you must shout from the rooftops every sinful desire you have but the vast majority of us don’t share any struggles, sin, or temptation with one another.

Third, Crucifixions are going to last awhile. Crucifixion victims usually stayed alive in painful agony for a few days before taking their last breath. On this side of heaven, our flesh is nailed to the cross but ultimately condemned.  One day, our King is coming back and making all things new. Then, our flesh will be fully buried and we can live in harmony with our God. Until then, crucifying your flesh is a daily process.

Now what? What is the use of crucifying our flesh? We don’t aim to only rid the evil within us. Our aim is a relationship with God. We can’t serve two masters; we either serve false idols through our flesh or the one True God through the Spirit.

## Be Guided by the Spirit

My high school marching band did something every year called a “blackout show”. It was our normal marching band show but we would tape glow sticks to our bodies and turn off all the lights in the stadium. This has a really cool effect where we look like a bunch of glowing skeletons marching about the field.

I was a part of the snare line and we had a fun tradition. One senior would turn around so his glowsticks weren’t seen, and march over to steal a freshman’s snare drum. This would greatly confuse the freshman who had no clue what was happening and it was too dark for him to see what was going on.

I bring this up because from a student’s perspective, we have to face forward and we can’t see anybody ahead of us. We had to know our part well enough to do the show without being able to see much of anything. We also had to trust our fellow students to do their part otherwise collisions would happen on the field. 

*\[Gal 5:25 ESV\] If we live by the Spirit, let us also keep in step with the Spirit.*

The word “step” here was referencing a military march. God will give us our marching orders and we must follow our part. Like the blackout show, God is in the pressbox way above crafting a beautiful show. We can only see a handful of others in this marching show. We must have faith in God’s orders. We must have faith in our fellow brothers and sisters to do their part.

Whether you are 9 or 99, if you’re still on this Earth, God has a plan and a purpose for you. What are your marching orders? Maybe you need to stop slacking on your prayer time? Maybe you need to tell your coworker or classmate about Jesus? Maybe he has called you to volunteer at one of the many great ministries in town? Pray that God convicts you and reveals the next steps of obedience you must take.

## Stay Humble

There’s a really funny image which shows a knight in full armor with no visible vulnerability. Until you notice the arrow sticking out of the eyeslit. Pride is that slit in our suit of armor. As you grow in your walk with Christ, the easier pride against your fellow believers will set in.

*\[Gal 5:26 ESV\] 26 Let us not become conceited, provoking one another, envying one another.*

When you give your life to Christ, you are painfully aware how inadequate you are. Jesus told the Woman of the Well to ”go sin no more”. As you grow closer to God, you will become a more heavenly creature. You will become more resistant to your fleshly desires and Satan’s attacks. However, ego is that arrow in the eye slit. 

Let’s break down the verse piece by piece.

*“Let us not become conceited”*

Conceited can also be translated as “seeking vain glory”. It is so easy to become prideful about our spiritual walk that we become Pharisees. We seek to flaunt how religious we are. Then we condemn fellow believers because they aren’t as “spiritual” as us. 

Another key word is “become”. We **become** conceited. Christians start out in humble repentance and then **become** conceited. Be weary of yourself and your pride.

“*Provoking one another*” 

Provoking is a symbol of challenging another to a fight. Our vain glory seeking will lead us to challenge one another. “Pastor Steve said this today and I boldly corrected him afterwards because it actually says this\! I study my Bible very often.” “Mhm, did you hear about Jenny drinking again? I’m only telling you so you can pray, it's not gossip. I’ve been her fervent prayer warrior during my daily prayer sessions.” 

Stop provoking your fellow believer and using your spirituality as a cover up. If Steve does say something wrong, then pull him aside and humbly tell him “you said X, I think the Bible says Y. Can we discuss this?” Don’t use your disagreement to show how smart you are. Do pray for each other. Don’t use prayer time as gossip time.

“*Envying one another”*

God gives us all a role to play, no role is better than another. I’m not greater than y’all just because I’m here and you’re there. We can play stupid church games and act like some roles are better than others. A pastor isn’t greater than the mechanic because one role is “spiritual” and the other isn’t. 

Josh, who owns the Autocraft mechanic shop down the street, is a Christian. I’m fully confident that God uses his shop to bless thousands of people. Even though he isn't in a “spiritual” role, the honest, hard-working mechanic gives glory to God. Whatever role God has you to play in life, giving an honest, hard-day's work gives God great glory.
